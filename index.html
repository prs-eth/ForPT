Methodology

We investigate two pretraining strategies for a common LitePT backbone — self-supervised and supervised — and transfer each to downstream forestry tasks via frozen features or fine-tuning, compared against training from scratch. This unified protocol enables a systematic comparison of representation learning strategies under identical transfer conditions and annotation budgets.

Self-Distillation Pipeline

The student encodes masked, partial views of a forest point cloud; the teacher encodes the same scene in full. Two prediction heads align the student's representations with the momentum-updated teacher's — with no labels and no negative pairs.

Self-Distillation Loss
→
Local-to-global Distillation
The teacher encodes a global view and its local-global head predicts target Ptl; the student encodes a local view and predicts Psl with the same head.
Ll2g = Σ H(Ptl, Psl)
→
Masked-to-global Distillation
The teacher encodes the unmasked global view and its mask-global head predicts Ptm; the student encodes the corresponding masked global view and predicts Psm.
Lm2g = Σ H(Ptm, Psm)

State-of-the-art semantic segmentation

We evaluate 3-class point-wise segmentation (ground / wood / leaf) on held-out forest plots, comparing a training-free kNN probe against linear probing, decoder probing, training from scratch, and full fine-tuning of the pretrained backbone.

Data efficiency
kNN alone scores decently with zero training; fine-tuning beats scratch throughout, and pretraining's edge is largest when labels are scarce.
Training dynamics
Pretraining accelerates convergence — fine-tuning for just 10 epochs already beats scratch trained for the full 100.
Qualitative comparison
Even the training-free kNN probe (leftmost column) produces decent segmentations that closely track ground truth across all three held-out plots — NIBIO, NIBIO_MLS, BlueCat.
Qualitative forest semantic segmentation results on FOR-instanceV2 test set samples when 0.81 ha labelled training data is used.

State-of-the-art species classification

We evaluate individual-tree species classification on the FOR-species20K benchmark (34 species).

Benchmark comparison
LitePT-scratch and ForPT-finetune outperform all previous methods on this benchmark. LitePT-scratch achieves an overall accuracy of 82.7%, while fine-tuning the pretrained ForPT backbone further increases the accuracy to 83.2%, achieving the best performance across all reported metrics.

State-of-the-art age regression

We evaluate individual-tree age regression on the FOR-age benchmark.

Benchmark comparison
LitePT-scratch achieves an RMSE of 20.41 years and an R2 of 0.74, already outperforming the previously reported PTv3-scratch and ForestFormer3D-finetune results. Fine-tuning the pretrained ForPT backbone improves the performance further, reducing the RMSE to 20.03 years and increasing R2 to 0.75, thereby achieving the best overall performance.

Evaluating supervised pretraining

We probe frozen representations from four task-specific supervised pretrained backbones (semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, species classification, age regression) on every other downstream task, and compare them against our self-supervised (SSL) pretrained backbone under the same protocol.

Transfer consistency
Across all four downstream tasks, self-supervised pretraining consistently produces the most transferable representations, outperforming task-specific supervised pretraining under the same probing protocol.
